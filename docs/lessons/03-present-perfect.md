## Lesson goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to connect earlier events with now, talk about experiences and present results, describe continuing states, use **since** and **for**, distinguish **been** from **gone**, and choose between the Present Perfect and Past Simple.

## 1. What Is It?

The Present Perfect describes an earlier event or state with a present connection. The exact past time is often unknown, unimportant, not mentioned or part of an unfinished period.

> **Subject + have/has + past participle (V3)**

- I have finished my work.
- She has lost her keys.
- We have visited Rome.
- They have lived here for six years.

Compare:

- **I lost my keys yesterday.** The finished past time is stated.
- **I have lost my keys.** The present result matters: I do not have them now.

Ask: **Is the connection with now more important than the finished past time?** If yes, the Present Perfect may be the right choice.

## 2. When Do We Use It?

### Past action with a present result

- I have lost my phone. *(I cannot use it now.)*
- She has broken her glasses. *(They are broken now.)*
- We have missed the bus. *(It has left.)*
- He has forgotten his password. *(He cannot log in.)*

### Life experience up to now

- I have visited Italy three times.
- Have you ever travelled alone?
- She has never flown in a helicopter.
- We have tried Japanese food.

Do not add a finished time:

- Correct: I have visited Paris.
- Correct: I visited Paris in 2023.
- Incorrect: I have visited Paris in 2023.

### Events in an unfinished time period

- I have answered fifteen emails today.
- We have received many orders this week.
- He has read four books this month.
- They have made excellent progress this year.

At 11 a.m., “I have had two meetings this morning” is natural. At 8 p.m., the morning is finished, so say “I had two meetings this morning.”

### States that began earlier and continue now

- I have known Maria for ten years.
- She has lived here since 2020.
- We have owned this house for a long time.
- They have been married for twenty-five years.

### Recent events and news

- She has just arrived.
- The meeting has started.
- They have already announced the results.
- Scientists have discovered a new species.

The Present Perfect can introduce news; the Past Simple can then give finished details:

> Our team has won the competition! They played brilliantly and scored twice in the second half.

### Achievements, changes and repeated actions up to now

- Technology has changed the way we work.
- Your English has improved greatly.
- I have called her three times today.
- They have won five matches this season.

## 3. Structure / Formula

### Positive

> **Subject + have/has + V3**

| Subject | Auxiliary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | have | We have finished. |
| He / She / It | has | She has finished. |

### Past participles

Regular past participles normally end in **-ed**. Irregular forms must be learned.

| Base | Past Simple | V3 |
|---|---|---|
| work | worked | worked |
| be | was/were | been |
| break | broke | broken |
| do | did | done |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| forget | forgot | forgotten |
| go | went | gone |
| know | knew | known |
| see | saw | seen |
| take | took | taken |
| write | wrote | written |

After **have/has**, always use V3:

- I have **seen** it—not *have saw*.
- She has **written** the email—not *has wrote*.
- They have **gone** home—not *have went*.

## 4. Positive Sentences

- I have completed the report.
- You have made good progress.
- He has left the office.
- She has written an email.
- The rain has stopped.
- We have booked the hotel.
- They have arrived safely.

### Contractions

| Full form | Contraction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I have | I’ve | I’ve finished. |
| You have | You’ve | You’ve improved. |
| He/She has | He’s/She’s | She’s arrived. |
| We/They have | We’ve/They’ve | They’ve gone home. |

**He’s** may mean **he is** or **he has**. The following verb reveals the meaning:

- He’s working. = He is working.
- He’s finished. = He has finished.

## 5. Negative Sentences

> **Subject + have/has + not + V3**

- I haven’t eaten yet.
- You haven’t answered my question.
- He hasn’t called today.
- She hasn’t completed the course.
- We haven’t seen that film.
- They haven’t arrived yet.

**Never** is already negative:

- Correct: I have never visited Canada.
- Incorrect: I haven’t never visited Canada.

## 6. Questions

### Yes/No questions

> **Have/Has + subject + V3?**

- Have you finished?
- Has she called?
- Have they arrived?
- Has Daniel sent the email?

Short answers:

- Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t.
- Yes, she has. / No, she hasn’t.

Do not contract positive short answers: **Yes, she has**, not **Yes, she’s**.

### WH-questions

> **WH-word + have/has + subject + V3?**

- What have you done?
- Where has she gone?
- Why have they cancelled the meeting?
- How many books have you read this year?
- How long have you lived here?

When **who** is the subject:

- Who has called?
- Who has broken the window?

## 7. Important Time Expressions

### Ever and never

- Have you ever visited Egypt?
- What is the best book you have ever read?
- I have never travelled alone.

### Just

Place it between the auxiliary and V3:

- I have just finished.
- She has just arrived.

### Already

- I have already paid.
- She has already completed the task.
- Have you finished already?

### Yet

Use it mainly at the end of questions and negatives:

- Have you finished yet?
- I haven’t finished yet.

### Recently, lately, so far and until now

- I have recently started a new course.
- Have you spoken to him lately?
- We have completed six lessons so far.
- Everything has gone well until now.

## 8. Since and For

### Since = starting point

- since Monday
- since 2020
- since childhood
- since I moved here

Examples:

- I have lived here since 2020.
- She has known him since university.
- We have been busy since Monday.

### For = duration

- for two hours
- for three days
- for six years
- for a long time

Examples:

- I have lived here for five years.
- She has known him for a long time.
- We have waited for thirty minutes.

Ask about duration with **How long + have/has + subject + V3?**

## 9. Been and Gone

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| has gone to | went and is still there or travelling | Maria has gone to the bank. |
| has been to | visited and returned | Maria has been to the bank. |
| has been in | is there for a duration | She has been in London for two weeks. |

## 10. Examples in Real Life

### Home

- I have cleaned the kitchen.
- Someone has eaten the last piece of cake.
- We haven’t decided what to cook yet.

### Work

- I have sent the invoice.
- The company has hired three employees this month.
- Have you completed the report yet?

### Study

- I have learned twenty words this week.
- She has passed all her exams.
- How much of the book have you read?

### Travel and technology

- Have you ever travelled alone?
- Our flight has just landed.
- I have forgotten my password.
- Have you installed the update yet?

## 11. Common Mistakes

**Wrong:** I have went there.  
**Correct:** I have gone there.  
**Why:** Use V3 after have/has.

**Wrong:** She has finished the report yesterday.  
**Correct:** She finished the report yesterday.  
**Why:** Yesterday is a finished past time.

**Wrong:** He have completed the task.  
**Correct:** He has completed the task.

**Wrong:** I am here since Monday.  
**Correct:** I have been here since Monday.

**Wrong:** I have lived here since five years.  
**Correct:** I have lived here for five years.

**Wrong:** I haven’t never tried sushi.  
**Correct:** I have never tried sushi.

For a living writer, “She has written ten novels” describes achievement up to now. For a writer who died, use “She wrote ten novels” because the person’s life is a finished period.

## 12. Compare It: Present Perfect vs Past Simple

| Present Perfect | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| connection with now | finished past time |
| I’ve broken my glasses. | I broke them last week. |
| Have you ever visited Turkey? | Did you visit Turkey last summer? |
| I have spoken to Sarah twice today. | I spoke to Sarah twice yesterday. |
| He has worked here for five years. *(still here)* | He worked there for five years. *(finished)* |

Decision guide:

1. Clearly finished past-time expression? Use Past Simple.
2. Exact time unknown or unimportant? Consider Present Perfect.
3. Present result? Use Present Perfect.
4. Time period still open? Use Present Perfect.
5. Situation continued until now? Use Present Perfect.

Informal American English sometimes uses Past Simple with **just, already** and **yet** where British English often prefers Present Perfect. Both may be natural in their varieties.

## 13. Quick Practice

1. She ________ just ________ the office. *(leave)*  
   **Answer:** has just left

2. I ________ that film three times: **saw / have seen / have saw**.  
   **Answer:** have seen

3. We have lived here ________ six years.  
   **Answer:** for

4. He has worked from home ________ 2022.  
   **Answer:** since

5. Make negative: She has completed the application.  
   **Answer:** She hasn’t completed the application.

6. Make a question: They have visited London.  
   **Answer:** Have they visited London?

7. Correct: I have went to that restaurant before.  
   **Answer:** I have gone to that restaurant before.

8. I ________ my keys. I cannot open the door. *(lose)*  
   **Answer:** have lost

9. Ahmed is still in Dubai. Ahmed has ________ to Dubai.  
   **Answer:** gone

10. Order: already / has / Daniel / finished / the report  
    **Answer:** Daniel has already finished the report.
