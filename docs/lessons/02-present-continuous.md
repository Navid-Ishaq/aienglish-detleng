## Lesson goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to describe activities in progress, temporary situations, changes and future arrangements; form sentences with **be + V-ing**; apply spelling rules; and distinguish dynamic meanings from states.

## 1. What Is It?

The Present Continuous—also called the Present Progressive—shows an activity or situation as happening, continuing or developing around the present time.

It does not always mean this exact second. It may describe a wider current period that is still unfinished.

- Right now: Please wait. I’m speaking to a customer.
- Around now: I’m reading a new book this week.
- Temporary: She’s staying with her sister this month.

## 2. When Do We Use It?

### Happening right now

- Please be quiet. The baby is sleeping.
- Look! It is raining.
- I’m talking to a customer right now.

### Happening around now

- I’m reading an interesting book this week.
- She’s taking an online course.
- We’re working on a new project.

### Temporary situations

- I’m staying with my brother for a few days.
- He’s working from home this month.
- They’re living in a hotel while their flat is repaired.

### Changing or developing situations

- Your English is improving.
- The weather is getting warmer.
- More people are working remotely.

### Repeated behaviour with emotion

With **always** or **constantly**, the continuous can show annoyance, surprise or admiration.

- He’s always losing his keys!
- You’re constantly interrupting me.
- She’s always helping other people.

### Future arrangements

Use it for a plan that is already arranged, often with a time, place or another person.

- I’m meeting the manager at ten tomorrow.
- We’re flying to Rome on Friday.
- Are you doing anything this weekend?

Words such as **now, today, this week, always** and **tomorrow** are clues, not automatic tense selectors. Context controls the tense.

## 3. Structure / Formula

> **Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing**

| Subject | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am + V-ing | I am working. |
| He / She / It | is + V-ing | She is working. |
| You / We / They | are + V-ing | They are working. |

### V-ing spelling

| Pattern | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Most verbs | add **-ing** | work → working; study → studying |
| Final silent **e** | remove **e** | make → making; write → writing |
| Short stressed vowel + consonant | double final consonant | run → running; sit → sitting |
| Final **ie** | change **ie** to **y** | lie → lying; die → dying |
| Final **c** | add **k** before **-ing** | panic → panicking |

Keep the **e** in **seeing, agreeing** and **being**. Do not double **w, x** or **y**: **snowing, fixing, playing**.

Stress matters in longer verbs: **begin → beginning**, but **open → opening**.

## 4. Positive Sentences

> **Subject + am/is/are + V-ing**

- I am preparing dinner.
- You are learning quickly.
- He is talking to a customer.
- Maya is writing a report.
- The company is expanding.
- We are waiting for the bus.
- They are playing football.

### Natural contractions

| Full form | Contraction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I’m | I’m working. |
| You are | You’re | You’re learning. |
| He/She/It is | He’s/She’s/It’s | She’s waiting. |
| We/They are | We’re/They’re | They’re travelling. |

## 5. Negative Sentences

> **Subject + am/is/are + not + V-ing**

- I’m not using the computer.
- You aren’t listening to me.
- He isn’t feeling well today.
- She isn’t working this week.
- We aren’t staying for dinner.
- They aren’t coming to the meeting.

Contractions:

- is not → isn’t
- are not → aren’t
- I am not → I’m not

Standard English normally does not use **I amn’t**.

## 6. Questions

> **Am/Is/Are + subject + V-ing?**

- Are you working? — Yes, I am. / No, I’m not.
- Is she studying? — Yes, she is. / No, she isn’t.
- Are they coming? — Yes, they are. / No, they aren’t.

### WH-questions

> **WH-word + am/is/are + subject + V-ing?**

- What are you doing?
- Where is he going?
- When are you leaving?
- Why are they laughing?

When **who** is the subject:

- Who is calling?
- Who is using the meeting room?

Positive short answers keep the full auxiliary: **Yes, I am**, not **Yes, I’m**.

## 7. Examples in Real Life

### Daily life

- I’m making breakfast.
- The children are getting ready for school.
- My neighbour is painting his house.

### Work and study

- We’re preparing the monthly report.
- Sara is speaking to a client.
- I’m taking an online course this month.

### Travel and plans

- We’re staying near the city centre.
- Our flight is leaving at six tomorrow morning.
- Are you travelling alone?

### Technology and change

- The computer is installing an update.
- More companies are using artificial intelligence.
- The business is growing quickly.

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Wrong:** She working now.  
**Correct:** She is working now.  
**Why:** The tense needs **am, is** or **are**.

**Wrong:** They are play football.  
**Correct:** They are playing football.  
**Why:** Use V-ing after the form of **be**.

**Wrong:** Does she working today?  
**Correct:** Is she working today?  
**Why:** Continuous questions use **be**, not do/does.

**Wrong:** He doesn’t working.  
**Correct:** He isn’t working.  
**Why:** The negative form is **is not + V-ing**.

**Wrong:** What she is doing?  
**Correct:** What is she doing?  
**Why:** Put the auxiliary before the subject.

**Wrong:** He is runing.  
**Correct:** He is running.  
**Why:** Double the final consonant.

### Stative verbs

Verbs such as **know, understand, believe, want, need, own** and **belong** are not normally continuous when they describe states.

- I know the answer—not *I am knowing the answer*.
- She owns the house—not *She is owning the house*.

Some verbs can be dynamic when their meaning changes:

| State meaning | Dynamic meaning |
|---|---|
| I think you’re right. *(believe)* | I’m thinking about the problem. *(mental activity)* |
| I have a car. *(possess)* | I’m having lunch. *(activity)* |
| The soup tastes good. | The chef is tasting the soup. |
| He is rude. *(general character)* | He is being rude. *(temporary behaviour)* |

## 9. Compare It: Present Simple vs Present Continuous

| Present Simple | Present Continuous |
|---|---|
| normal or repeated | current or temporary |
| I work from home. | I’m working from home this week. |
| He drinks coffee every morning. | He’s drinking coffee now. |
| She always asks questions. *(neutral habit)* | She’s always asking the same question! *(emotion)* |
| The train leaves at seven. *(timetable)* | We’re leaving at seven. *(arrangement)* |

## 10. Quick Practice

1. Please be quiet. The baby ________. *(sleep)*  
   **Answer:** is sleeping

2. I ________ with my sister while my apartment is repaired. *(stay)*  
   **Answer:** am staying

3. Choose: Look! It **rains / is raining / raining**.  
   **Answer:** is raining

4. Make negative: She is driving to work today.  
   **Answer:** She isn’t driving to work today.

5. Make a question: They are staying at the hotel.  
   **Answer:** Are they staying at the hotel?

6. Correct: Does she studying at the moment?  
   **Answer:** Is she studying at the moment?

7. “I am living in Valencia this year” suggests **permanent / temporary / finished**.  
   **Answer:** temporary

8. My father usually ________ to work, but today he ________ the train. *(drive/take)*  
   **Answer:** drives; is taking

9. The train ________ at 8:15 tomorrow, and we ________ the others at the station. *(leave/meet)*  
   **Answer:** leaves; are meeting
