## 📘 1. What Is It?

The Present Simple presents something as **generally true, normal, regular or repeated**. It does not usually describe an action happening at this exact moment.

- ✅ I walk to work every morning.
- ✅ Water boils at 100°C.
- ✅ She works in a hospital.
- ✅ They live near the station.

> [!NOTE]
> Think of it as the tense for what is **normally true across time** — not what is happening right now.

**Signal words:** `always` `usually` `often` `sometimes` `rarely` `never` `every day` `on Mondays` `once a week`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These time expressions are useful clues, but **meaning — not a single keyword — chooses the tense.**

## 🧩 3. Structure / Formula

> <kbd>Subject</kbd> + <kbd>base verb</kbd> <sub>(+ **-s / -es** with he, she, it)</sub>

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | `work` | They work here. |
| He / She / It | `works` | She works here. |

### ✍️ Third-person singular spelling

| Pattern | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Most verbs | add `-s` | work → works · read → reads |
| Ends in `-s -sh -ch -x -o` | add `-es` | watch → watches · go → goes |
| Consonant + `y` | `y` → `ies` | study → studies · carry → carries |
| Vowel + `y` | add `-s` | play → plays · enjoy → enjoys |
| Irregular | learn separately | have → has · be → is |

The verb **be** breaks the pattern completely:

> <kbd>I</kbd> **am** &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>You / We / They</kbd> **are** &nbsp;·&nbsp; <kbd>He / She / It</kbd> **is**

## ✅ 4. Positive Sentences

- I work from home on Fridays.
- You speak English clearly.
- He drives to work.
- She teaches mathematics.
- The machine makes a strange noise.
- We study together.
- They play football after work.

> [!WARNING]
> Never add **-s** to the verb after **I, you, we** or **they**.

## 🚫 5. Negative Sentences

> <kbd>Subject</kbd> + <kbd>do / does</kbd> + <kbd>not</kbd> + <kbd>base verb</kbd>

- I do not work on Sundays.
- You don't need an appointment.
- He does not live here.
- She doesn't drive to work.
- We don't agree. · They don't speak French.

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She doesn't **works** here. | She doesn't **work** here. | After does/doesn't, the main verb returns to its base form. |

With **be**, never add do/does:

- I am not tired.
- He isn't at home.
- They aren't ready.

## ❓ 6. Questions

**Yes/No questions**

> <kbd>Do / Does</kbd> + <kbd>subject</kbd> + <kbd>base verb</kbd>?

- Do you work here? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
- Does she speak Spanish? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
- Do they live nearby?

**WH-questions**

> <kbd>WH-word</kbd> + <kbd>do / does</kbd> + <kbd>subject</kbd> + <kbd>base verb</kbd>?

- Where do you live?
- What does he do?
- Why does she study English?
- When do they arrive?

> [!NOTE]
> When the question word **is** the subject, skip do/does: *Who works here? · What causes this problem?*

Questions with **be**: *Are you ready? · Is she at work? · Where are they?*

## ⚠️ 8. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She **work** here. | She **works** here. | Add -s/-es with he, she and it. |
| He **don't** like coffee. | He **doesn't** like coffee. | Use doesn't with he, she and it. |
| She doesn't **works** here. | She doesn't **work** here. | Use the base verb after does/doesn't. |
| Does he **lives** nearby? | Does he **live** nearby? | The main verb stays base form after does. |
| I **am knowing** the answer. | I **know** the answer. | Know normally describes a state. |
| I **am agree**. | I **agree**. | Agree is a main verb, not an adjective used with be. |

## ⚖️ 9. Compare It: Present Simple vs Present Continuous

| 🟢 Present Simple | 🟠 Present Continuous |
|---|---|
| Normal, repeated or generally true | Current, temporary or developing |
| I work from home. | I'm working from home this week. |
| He drinks coffee every morning. | He's drinking coffee now. |
| She teaches English. | She is teaching English this semester. |
| The train leaves at seven *(fixed timetable)* | We're leaving at seven *(personal arrangement)* |
