Present Perfect

Connect an earlier event or state with now—through results, experiences, unfinished time and situations that continue.

1 What Is It?

Build a bridge from before to now

The Present Perfect describes an earlier event or state with a present connection. The exact past time is often unknown, unimportant or part of a period that is still open.

Before nowAn event happened or a state began.
→
NOWThe result, experience or situation matters now.
I lost my keys yesterday.A finished event with a finished time.
I have lost my keys.Present result: I do not have them now.
Useful question: Is the connection with now more important than the finished past time? If yes, the Present Perfect may be the right choice.
2 When Do We Use It?

Choose it for the present connection

Present result

A past action has an important result now.

She has broken her glasses.
We have missed the bus.

Life experience

Something has or has not happened during life until now.

Have you ever visited Canada?
I have never tried skiing.

Unfinished time

The time period still includes the present.

I have had two meetings today.
We have gained three clients this month.

Continuing state

A state began earlier and is still true.

I have known her for ten years.
He has lived here since 2020.

Recent news

A recent event matters now.

She has just arrived.
The company has opened a new office.

Change or achievement

Progress has developed up to now.

Your English has improved.
Technology has changed our lives.
everneveralreadyyetjustrecentlylatelyso farsincefor

Time perspective matters: “I’ve had two meetings this morning” is natural while the morning continues. Later, say “I had two meetings this morning.”

4 Positive Sentences

Say what has happened or remained true

Subject + have / has + V3

I have completed the report.

You have made good progress.

He has left the office.

She has written an email.

We have booked the hotel.

They have arrived safely.

Natural contractions

Full formContractionExample

Read the next verb: “He’s working” means he is; “He’s finished” means he has.

5 Negative Sentences

Put not after have or has

Subject + have / has + not + V3

I haven’t finished the report.

She hasn’t replied yet.

We haven’t visited that museum.

He hasn’t found his keys.

They haven’t arrived.

have not → haven’tWe haven’t eaten yet.
has not → hasn’tShe hasn’t called today.

Never is already negative: say “I have never visited Canada,” not “I haven’t never visited Canada.”

8 Common Mistakes

Protect the bridge to now

Wrong: I have went there.

Correct: I have gone there.

Why? Use V3 after have or has.

Wrong: She has finished it yesterday.

Correct: She finished it yesterday.

Why? Yesterday is a finished past time.

Wrong: He have completed the task.

Correct: He has completed the task.

Why? Use has with he, she and it.

Wrong: I am here since Monday.

Correct: I have been here since Monday.

Why? The state began earlier and continues now.

Wrong: I have lived here since five years.

Correct: I have lived here for five years.

Why? For introduces a duration; since introduces a starting point.

Since and for

since + starting pointsince Monday · since 2020 · since I moved
for + durationfor two hours · for five years · for a long time

Been and gone

9 Compare It

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Connection with now?Choose Present Perfect.
OR
Finished past time?Choose Past Simple.
PRESENT RESULT

I’ve broken my glasses.

They are broken now.

FINISHED EVENT

I broke them last week.

The time is stated and finished.

EXPERIENCE

Have you ever visited Turkey?

At any time until now.

SPECIFIC TRIP

Did you visit Turkey last summer?

A specific finished time.

STILL TRUE

He has worked here for five years.

He still works here.

FINISHED STATE

He worked there for five years.

He does not work there now.

Language note: Informal American English sometimes uses Past Simple with just, already and yet where British English often prefers Present Perfect. Both may be natural in their varieties.

10 Quick Practice

Connect form, time and meaning

FILL IN THE BLANK

She ________ just ________ the office. (leave)

has just left — a very recent event.
CHOOSE THE FORM

I ________ that film three times: A) saw · B) have seen · C) have saw

B) have seen — experience up to now.
SINCE OR FOR?

We have lived here ________ six years.

for — six years is a duration.
SINCE OR FOR?

He has worked from home ________ 2022.

since — 2022 is a starting point.
MAKE NEGATIVE

She has completed the application.

She hasn’t completed the application.
MAKE A QUESTION

They have visited London.

Have they visited London?
CORRECT THE MISTAKE

I have went to that restaurant before.

I have gone to that restaurant before.
PERFECT OR PAST?

I ________ my keys. I cannot open the door. (lose)

have lost — the present result matters.
BEEN OR GONE?

Ahmed is still in Dubai. Ahmed has ________ to Dubai.

gone — he has not returned.
WORD ORDER

already / has / Daniel / finished / the report

Daniel has already finished the report.
