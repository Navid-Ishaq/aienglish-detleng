Present Simple

Use the Present Simple for habits, routines, facts, states and things that happen repeatedly.

1 What Is It?

The tense for what is generally true

The Present Simple presents something as a fact, a normal situation or a repeated part of life. It does not focus on an action happening right now.

PastNOWFuture

A routine can repeat before, around and after now: “I study every evening.”

2 When Do We Use It?

Choose it for the context—not only a signal word

Habits and routines

Use it for actions that form a regular pattern.

I check my email after breakfast.
She studies after dinner.

Facts and general truths

Use it for information presented as generally true.

Water boils at 100°C at standard pressure.
The Earth moves around the Sun.

States

Use it for thoughts, feelings, possession and other states.

I know the answer.
He owns a small apartment.

Repeated events

Use it when an event happens again and again.

The shop closes at six every day.
Our team meets on Fridays.

Schedules and timetables

A fixed future schedule can use the Present Simple.

The train leaves at 7:30 tomorrow.
The course starts next Monday.

Instructions and time clauses

It appears in instructions and after words such as when or until.

First, you open the file.
I’ll call you when I arrive.
3 Structure / Formula

Build the Present Simple

V1 means the base form of the verb: work, go, study.

I / You / We / They + V1
He / She / It + V1-s / -es

Most verbs

work → works
read → reads
play → plays

Spelling changes

watch → watches
go → goes
study → studies
have → has

The verb be is different

I am · You / We / They are · He / She / It is
4 Positive Sentences

Say what happens or is true

Subject + V1 (add -s / -es for he, she, it)
I work from home on Fridays.
You speak very clearly.
Maya teaches English online.
The bus arrives every twenty minutes.
We have lunch together.
They live near the station.
5 Negative Sentences

Use don’t or doesn’t + the base verb

I / You / We / They + do not (don’t) + V1
He / She / It + does not (doesn’t) + V1

Important: after doesn’t, use the base verb. The third-person marker is already inside does: “She doesn’t work,” not “doesn’t works.”

I don’t drink coffee after lunch.
You don’t need an appointment.
He doesn’t drive to work.
The shop doesn’t open on Sundays.
We don’t agree with that idea.
They don’t live here.

Negative forms of be

I am not · You/We/They are not (aren’t) · He/She/It is not (isn’t)

6 Questions

Put do or does before the subject

Do + I / you / we / they + V1 …?
Does + he / she / it + V1 …?
TypeQuestionShort answerFull answer
Yes / NoDo you work nearby?Yes, I do.Yes, I work nearby.
Yes / NoDoes she like coffee?No, she doesn’t.No, she doesn’t like coffee.
WH-Where do they live?—They live in Valencia.
WH-When does the class start?—It starts at nine.

Remember: “Does she like…?” The verb stays in its base form after does.

8 Common Mistakes

Notice what changes—and what does not

Wrong: She work every day.

Correct: She works every day.

Why? Add -s to the verb with she in an affirmative sentence.

Wrong: He doesn’t works here.

Correct: He doesn’t work here.

Why? Doesn’t already carries the third-person marking, so use V1 after it.

Wrong: Does she likes coffee?

Correct: Does she like coffee?

Why? Use the base verb after does.

Wrong: I am knowing him.

Correct: I know him.

Why? Know normally describes a state in this meaning, so the Present Simple is natural.

9 Compare It

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Use the Present Simple for the normal fact or routine. Use the Present Continuous when you view an activity as in progress, current or temporary.

Normal / routine

Present Simple

I work from home.

This is my normal working situation.

She teaches English.

This is her regular occupation.

Current / temporary

Present Continuous

I am working from home this week.

This is a temporary situation now.

She is teaching English this semester.

This is her current, limited-period activity.
