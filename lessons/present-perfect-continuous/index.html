1 What Is It?

Follow the activity towards now

The Present Perfect Continuous connects an earlier activity with the present. The activity may still be continuing, or it may have just stopped while its result remains visible or relevant.

Activity across a period of time
STARTNOW
Began earlierContinued or repeatedReaches or affects now
Still continuingI have been reading for two hours.
Recently stoppedI’m tired because I have been running.

Core focus: the activity, duration, ongoing effort, repetition or present evidence—not simply a completed result.

2 When Do We Use It?

Choose it when the process matters

Continuing activity

It began earlier and is still happening.

She has been working since eight.
We have been waiting for thirty minutes.

Duration

The length of the activity is important.

I have been reading for two hours.
They have been travelling all month.

Present evidence

The activity recently stopped, but its evidence remains.

The ground is wet. It has been raining.
You’re out of breath. Have you been running?

Repeated activity

It stopped and restarted within a period leading to now.

I have been calling him all morning.
She has been going to the gym regularly.

Temporary situation

The ongoing situation is not viewed as permanent.

I have been working from home this month.
They have been staying in a hotel.

Change or development

A trend has continued towards the present.

Your English has been improving.
Prices have been rising.

3 Structure / Formula

Build all four essential parts

Subject + have / has + been + V-ing

SubjectComplete verb phraseExample

He / She / Ithas been + V-ingShe has been studying.

V-ing reminder

PatternExamples
Add -ingwork → working; study → studying
Remove final silent emake → making; write → writing
Double final consonantrun → running; begin → beginning
Change ie to ylie → lying; die → dying

Complete chain: “She has been studying.” Do not lose has, been or -ing.

Keep the activity flowing

Subject + have / has + been + V-ing

It has been raining.

Natural contractions

Full formContractionExample
I have beenI’ve beenI’ve been waiting.
He / She has beenHe’s / She’s beenShe’s been studying.
We / They have beenWe’ve / They’ve beenThey’ve been travelling.

“She’s working” means she is working. “She’s been working” means she has been working. The word been reveals the meaning.

5 Negative Sentences

Put not after have or has

Subject + have / has + not + been + V-ing

I haven’t been sleeping well.

You haven’t been listening to me.

He hasn’t been working today.

She hasn’t been feeling well lately.

We haven’t been using the old system.

They haven’t been studying regularly.

have not → haven’tWe haven’t been waiting for long.
has not → hasn’tHe hasn’t been attending the meetings.

8 Common Mistakes

Keep every part in the chain

Wrong: I have been work all day.

Correct: I have been working all day.

Why? Use V-ing after have/has been.

Wrong: She has being studying.

Correct: She has been studying.

Why? The tense uses the past participle been.

Wrong: They have working since morning.

Correct: They have been working since morning.

Why? Do not leave out been.

Wrong: How long you have been studying?

Correct: How long have you been studying?

Why? Move have/has before the subject.

Wrong: I have been studying since three hours.

Correct: I have been studying for three hours.

Why? Use for with duration and since with a starting point.

Wrong: I have been knowing her for years.

Correct: I have known her for years.

Why? Know normally describes a state.

Stative meanings normally use the simple form

knowbelieveunderstandownbelongneedwantlove
StateI have had this car for five years. (possess)
Dynamic activityI have been having problems with it. (experience)
9 Compare It

Result or process?

Present Perfect SimpleResult · completion · quantity · state

I have written five emails.

VS
Present Perfect ContinuousProcess · duration · effort · evidence

I have been writing emails all morning.

COMPLETED RESULT

She has cleaned the kitchen.

The kitchen is clean.

ACTIVITY / EFFORT

She has been cleaning the kitchen.

The process matters; it may be unfinished.

QUANTITY

He has read three chapters.

The completed number matters.

DURATION

He has been reading for two hours.

The time and activity matter.

NUMBER OF ATTEMPTS

I have called him five times.

Count the completed calls.

REPEATED EFFORT

I have been calling him all morning.

Focus on the ongoing effort.

Present Continuous vs Present Perfect Continuous

I am studying English.Current activity; no starting time is given.
I have been studying English for two years.It began earlier and continues towards now.

With verbs such as live, work, teach and study, both perfect forms can sometimes be correct. The simple sounds more factual; the continuous highlights duration or ongoing experience.

CHOOSE THE FORM

SINCE OR FOR?

for — a duration.
MAKE NEGATIVE

She hasn’t been sleeping well.

How long have you been learning English?
CORRECT THE MISTAKE

She has being waiting for us.

She has been waiting for us.
RESULT OR PROCESS?

I ________ five emails this morning. (write)

have written — completed quantity.
RESULT OR PROCESS?

I ________ emails all morning. (write)

have been writing — activity and duration.
PRESENT EVIDENCE

Their clothes are dirty because they ________ outside. (play)

have been playing
STATE OR ACTIVITY?

I ________ this house for ten years. (own)

have owned — own describes a state.
NOW OR DURATION?

Please wait. I ________ to a customer right now. (talk)

am talking — current action.
NOW OR DURATION?

I ________ to customers since nine. (talk)

have been talking — past-to-present duration.
