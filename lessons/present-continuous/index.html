Present Continuous

Use the Present Continuous for activities in progress, temporary situations, developing changes and arranged future plans.

1 What Is It?

See an activity as in progress

The Present Continuous—also called the Present Progressive—shows an activity or situation as happening, continuing or developing around the present time.

It does not always mean this exact second. It can describe a wider current period that is still unfinished.

Activity in progress around now
NOW
Started earlierCurrent periodMay continue

“Please wait. I’m speaking to a customer.”

2 When Do We Use It?

Use meaning—not a single signal word

Happening right now

The action is in progress at the moment of speaking.

Please be quiet. The baby is sleeping.
Look! It is raining.
I’m talking to a customer right now.

Happening around now

The activity belongs to the current unfinished period, even if it is not happening this second.

I’m reading an interesting book this week.
She’s taking an online course.
We’re working on a new project.

Temporary situations

The situation is true for a limited time rather than permanently.

I’m staying with my brother for a few days.
He’s working from home this month.
They’re living in a hotel while their flat is repaired.

Changing situations

The speaker sees a change or development in progress.

Your English is improving.
The weather is getting warmer.
More people are working remotely.

Repeated behaviour with emotion

With always or constantly, the continuous can show annoyance, surprise or admiration.

He’s always losing his keys!
You’re constantly interrupting me.
She’s always helping other people.

Future arrangements

The plan is already arranged, often with a time, place or another person.

I’m meeting the manager at ten tomorrow.
We’re flying to Rome on Friday.
Are you doing anything this weekend?
Context controls the tense. Words such as now, today, this week, always and tomorrow are clues—not automatic tense selectors.
3 Structure / Formula

Build it with be + V-ing

Subject+am / is / are+verb-ing
I am working · He / She / It is working · You / We / They are working

Short stressed vowel + consonantDouble the final consonantrun → running

Final ieChange ie to ylie → lying

Keep the e when needed

see → seeing

Do not double w, x or y

snow → snowing

Stress matters in longer verbs: begin → beginning, prefer → preferring; but open → opening and visit → visiting.

4 Positive Sentences

Say what is happening or developing

Subject + am / is / are + V-ing
I am preparing dinner.
You are learning quickly.
He is talking to a customer.
Maya is writing a report.
The company is expanding.
We are waiting for the bus.
They are playing football.

Natural contractions

Full formContractionExample
I amI’mI’m working.
You areYou’reYou’re learning.
He / She / It isHe’s / She’s / It’sShe’s waiting outside.
We / They areWe’re / They’reThey’re travelling through Spain.

Notice: “She’s working” means she is working. In “She’s finished,” she’s may mean she has. The following verb form tells you which meaning is intended.

5 Negative Sentences

Put not after am, is or are

Subject + am / is / are + not + V-ing
I’m not using the computer.
You aren’t listening to me.
He isn’t feeling well today.
She isn’t working this week.
The machine isn’t operating correctly.
We aren’t staying for dinner.
They aren’t coming to the meeting.

is not

isn’t
He isn’t sleeping.

are not

aren’t
They aren’t waiting.

For I am not, the normal contraction is I’m not. Standard English normally does not use “I amn’t.”

6 Questions

Move am, is or are before the subject

Am / Is / Are + subject + V-ing?
TypeQuestionShort answerFull answer
Yes / NoAre you working?Yes, I am.Yes, I am working.
Yes / NoIs she studying?No, she isn’t.No, she isn’t studying.
Yes / NoAre they coming?Yes, they are.Yes, they are coming.
WH-What are you doing?—I’m preparing dinner.
WH-Where is he going?—He’s going to the station.
WH-When are you leaving?—We’re leaving tomorrow.

WH-question formula

WH-word + am / is / are + subject + V-ing?

Subject question: When who is the subject, say “Who is calling?” or “Who is using the meeting room?”

Positive short answers normally keep the full auxiliary: “Yes, I am,” not “Yes, I’m.”

8 Common Mistakes

Build the complete verb phrase

Wrong: She working now.

Correct: She is working now.

Why? The tense needs am, is or are before the -ing verb.

Wrong: They are play football.

Correct: They are playing football.

Why? Use V-ing after the form of be.

Wrong: Does she working today?

Correct: Is she working today?

Why? Present Continuous questions use be, not do or does.

Wrong: He doesn’t working.

Correct: He isn’t working.

Why? The negative form is is not + V-ing.

Wrong: What she is doing?

Correct: What is she doing?

Why? Put the auxiliary before the subject in this question.

Wrong: He is runing.

Correct: He is running.

Why? Double the final consonant: run → running.

Wrong: I am knowing the answer.

Correct: I know the answer.

Why? Know normally describes a state, so the Present Simple is natural.

Stative verbs: judge the meaning

Verbs such as know, understand, believe, want, need, own and belong are not normally continuous when they describe states. But some verbs can become dynamic when their meaning changes.

State meaningDynamic meaning
I think you’re right. (believe)I’m thinking about the problem. (mental activity)
I have a car. (possess)I’m having lunch. (activity)
The soup tastes good. (has a flavour)The chef is tasting the soup. (actively checking)
He is rude. (general character)He is being rude. (temporary behaviour)
9 Compare It

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Normal, repeated or generally true?Choose Present Simple.
OR
Current, temporary or developing?Choose Present Continuous.

Normal situation

I work from home.

This is my regular working situation.

Temporary situation

I’m working from home this week.

This is current and limited in time.

Routine

He drinks coffee every morning.

This happens regularly.

Action now

He’s drinking coffee now.

The activity is in progress.

Neutral repetition

She always asks questions.

This neutrally describes her habit.

Emotional repetition

She’s always asking the same question!

The speaker’s reaction is noticeable.

Timetable

The train leaves at seven tomorrow.

This is a fixed schedule.

Personal arrangement

We’re leaving at seven tomorrow.

This is our arranged plan.

10 Quick Practice

Make the meaning and form work together

Fill in the blank

Please be quiet. The baby ________. (sleep)

is sleeping — The activity is happening now.

Fill in the blank

I ________ with my sister while my apartment is being repaired. (stay)

am staying — This is a temporary current situation.

Choose the correct form

Look! It: A) rains · B) is raining · C) raining · D) is rain

B) is raining — Use is + V-ing for an action happening now.

Make negative

Change this sentence: She is driving to work today.

She isn’t driving to work today.

Make a question

Change this sentence into a question: They are staying at the hotel.

Are they staying at the hotel?

Correct the mistake

Does she studying at the moment?

Is she studying at the moment? Use is, not does.

Meaning check

“I am living in Valencia this year” suggests: A) certainly permanent · B) temporary or limited · C) finished past.

B) temporary or limited.

Simple or continuous?

My father usually ________ to work, but today he ________ the train. (drive / take)

My father usually drives to work, but today he is taking the train. The first is a routine; the second is today’s temporary situation.

Schedule or arrangement?

The train ________ at 8:15 tomorrow, and we ________ the others at the station. (leave / meet)

The train leaves at 8:15 tomorrow, and we are meeting the others at the station. The train is a timetable; the meeting is a personal arrangement.
